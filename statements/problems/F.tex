\begin{problem}{F}{Fun Floorplan Finish Friday}{1 second}

\noindent
Conan and the Detective Boys are spending their winter holiday at \textbf{Bug Castle}, a strange castle left in the care of Prof. Agasa by his legendary programmer friend, who finally decided to touch grass in Hawaii.

\noindent
Bug Castle has $N$ rooms connected by $N-1$ two-way passages, forming a tree. The kids start in room $1$. Prof. Agasa has prepared $N$ puzzles with distinct difficulty levels $1,2,\ldots,N$ and must place exactly one puzzle in each room.

\noindent
Whenever the kids visit a room for the first time, they must solve its puzzle. After that, they may choose any adjacent room to visit next. They must eventually solve all $N$ puzzles, and Prof. Agasa imposes one additional rule: \textbf{each passage may be traversed at most twice}.

\noindent
Prof. Agasa wants the kids to finish before Friday. Since the kids may choose which adjacent room to visit next, the same assignment can result in different difficulty sequences. Encountering difficult puzzles too early may discourage them.

\noindent
The \textbf{worst-case difficulty sequence} of an assignment is the \textbf{lexicographically largest} difficulty sequence the kids can encounter. Formally speaking, he wants an assignment whose worst-case difficulty sequence is not lexicographically larger than that of any other assignment.

\InputFile

\noindent
The first line contains an integer $T$ ($1 \le T \le 2 \cdot 10^5$) --- the number of test cases.

\noindent
For each test case:

\noindent
The first line contains an integer $N$ ($2 \le N \le 2\cdot 10^5$) --- the number of rooms.

\noindent
The second line contains $N-1$ integers $P_2,P_3,\ldots,P_N$ $(1 \le P_i < i)$, indicating that there is a passage between room $P_i$ and room $i$ for every $2 \le i \le N$.

\noindent
It is guaranteed that the sum of $N$ in all test cases does not exceed $2\cdot 10^5$

\OutputFile

\noindent
For each test case, output one line containing $N$ integers. The $i$-th integer should be the difficulty level of the puzzle assigned to room $i$.

\noindent
If there is more than one assignment whose worst-case difficulty sequence is \textbf{lexicographically smallest}, output any of them.
